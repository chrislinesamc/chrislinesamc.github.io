Project · co-author

DHARANI

A 3D atlas of the developing human brain, from 14 to 24 weeks of gestation. Scroll to watch five real fetal brains, reconstructed from serial sections, grow.

Scroll

Twenty microns at a time.

Each brain is cut into sections 20 µm thick, stained, scanned and reconstructed in 3D. What you are looking at is one of those reconstructions, rendered live in your browser.

14 weeks

A human brain in the second trimester. The lateral fissure and the calcarine, parieto-occipital and cingulate sulci have already begun.

17 weeks

A cell-sparse layer between the cortical plate and the subplate, carrying late-migrating neurons, has spread from the orbital cortex to the frontal cortex.

21 weeks

The cerebellar cortex is already foliated, and its nuclei and an early Purkinje cell layer appear.

22 weeks

Across these weeks, cytoarchitectonic boundaries become visible before the folds that will later mark them.

24 weeks

The central and postcentral sulci appear, and the cortical plate starts to form layers in the parietal and occipital cortex.

Open it anywhere.

The 3D reconstruction lets you view a fetal brain in all three planes, like an MRI, something histology of the fetal brain could not offer before.

Open and free

Explore it yourself.

Every section, the postmortem MRI and the annotations are online in the DHARANI viewer, and the 3D volumes are on AWS Open Data.

DHARANI: A 3D Developing Human-Brain Atlas Resource to Advance Neuroscience Internationally Integrated Multimodal Imaging and High-Resolution Histology of the Second Trimester

Verma R, Bota M, Ram K, … Sam C C, … Manger PR, Sivaprakasam M · all 29 authors Richa Verma, Mihail Bota, Keerthi Ram, Jaikishan Jayakumar, Rebecca Folkerth, Karthika Pandurangan, Jivitha Jyothi Ramesh, Moitrayee Majumder, Rakshika Raveendran, Reetuparna Nanda, Sivamani K, Amal Dhivahar S, Srinivasa Karthik, Ramdayalan Kumarasami, Suresh S, S Lata, E Harish Kumar, Rajeswaran Rangasami, Chitra Srinivasan, Jayaraman Kumutha, Sudha Vasudevan, Koushik Bhat, Chrisline Sam C, Sivathanu Neelakantan, Stephen Savoia, Partha P Mitra, Jayaraj Joseph, Paul R Manger, Mohanasankar Sivaprakasam
  • The first online platform with 3D histological reconstructions of the developing human brain, across five fetal brains from 14 to 24 weeks.
  • Sulci begin at 14 weeks with the lateral fissure and the calcarine, parieto-occipital and cingulate sulci; the central and postcentral sulci appear by 24 weeks, but cytoarchitectonic boundaries show before the folds.
  • Lamination of the cortical plate begins at 24 weeks in the parietal and occipital cortex. The frontal cortex is not yet laminated, though putative Betz cells are already visible.
  • Cerebellar nuclei and an early Purkinje cell layer appear by 21 weeks, in a cerebellar cortex that is already foliated.
