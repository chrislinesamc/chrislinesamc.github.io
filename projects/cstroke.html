Latest work · co-author

C-Stroke

The first whole-brain, cell-resolution reconstruction of a human ischemic stroke, registered to the patient's clinical MRI.

Scroll

11,009 sections

The patient lived 16 days after a middle cerebral artery occlusion. The fixed brain was then cut serially at 20 µm, section after section through the whole organ.

Ten stains.

Nissl, H&E and silver for myelin, and seven immunohistochemical markers: for the blood–brain barrier, hypoxia, axonal injury, axonal sprouting, astrocytes, microglia and the vasculature.

Back together, aligned with the MRI.

The sections were reconstructed in 3D and registered to the MRI taken in life, so what the scan showed can be compared, region by region, with what the cells show.

39% more injury than the scan showed

The injury seen in the tissue exceeded the lesion on diffusion MRI by 39%. Axonal injury and sprouting reached tissue up to 40 mm from the edge of the core.

1.5 PB of images

AI-based cell and pathology detection ran across the whole dataset, measuring the injury by MRI-defined lesion, arterial territory and brain region.

Open and online

Explore it yourself.

About 1,100 annotated, MRI-registered sections are open to explore. C-Stroke was released at IIT Madras by Prime Minister Narendra Modi in October 2026.

Preprint · 2026

A 3D whole-brain cellular atlas of MRI-mapped human ischemic stroke: Injury beyond the lesion defined by neuroimaging

Nanjappa R, Ayyappan A, Fathima M N, … Chrisline Sam C, … Ram K, Nauen DW, Folkerth RD, Sivaprakasam M · all 30 authors Rakesh Nanjappa, Aparna Ayyappan, Nishath Fathima M, Sriram Venkatesaperumal, Gayathri Kumaran, Sruti Rekha Muni, Shrimathi Mani, Roshan Kumar V.A.M, Chrisline Sam C, A.S.T Aditya Bharg, Hemanand I, Hisham Kadambot, Nathan Iyappan, Ritesh Choudhary V, Veni Paramasivan, Niteshkumar Sundhar, Supriti Mulay, Jaikishan Jaikumar, Sivathanu Neelakantan, Rajeswaran Rangaswami, Abi Manesh, Mousumi Sen, Ranjit Emmanuel James, George Varghese, Matthew P. Frosch, Richa Verma, Keerthi Ram, David W. Nauen, Rebecca D. Folkerth, Mohanasankar Sivaprakasam
  • The first whole-brain, cell-resolution histological reconstruction of human ischemic stroke registered to ante-mortem clinical MRI.
  • Clinical MRI underestimated the injury: the histologically defined injury exceeded the DWI lesion by 39%.
  • Axonal injury and sprouting extended through white matter up to 40 mm beyond the core, including a potentially novel population of regenerating axons around the infarct.
  • Blood–brain barrier disruption stayed within the acute infarct, while hypoxic changes reached distant cortex unseen on MRI, and subclinical pathology was present in the other hemisphere.
